# NavCRM — Project Overview

> Based on the public NavCRM website reviewed on **19 October 2026**. This document describes the product as presented publicly; it is not an audit of the software or confirmation that every advertised feature is available.

## What is NavCRM?

**NavCRM is a business management platform for service businesses in Tajikistan, developed by NavTech in Dushanbe.** It brings online appointment booking, customer records, payments, inventory, staff pay, loyalty and reporting into one system.

The product serves both sides of a service business: customers can find a business and book appointments, while owners and employees manage the work behind those appointments. Industry profiles adapt the system to different types of business.

Its main promise is straightforward: customers book themselves, routine calculations happen automatically, and the owner can follow the business from a phone.

Website: [navcrm.tj](https://navcrm.tj/)

## The problems it addresses

NavCRM is positioned as an alternative to managing a business through notebooks, spreadsheets, phone calls and separate messaging apps. The website focuses on:

- **Missed appointments:** reminders, confirmations, rescheduling and prepayments help reduce no-shows.
- **Lost booking opportunities:** customers can book outside working hours without waiting for an administrator.
- **Scattered customer information:** visits, purchases, notes and preferences live in a shared customer record.
- **Manual financial work:** payments, debts, payroll and stock movements connect to recorded transactions.
- **Limited owner visibility:** dashboards and reports show revenue, staff workload and customer activity.
- **Unreliable connectivity:** mobile offline operation lets staff continue working during an internet outage.

## Main product capabilities

### Booking and scheduling

- Online booking through links or entry points on Instagram, Telegram, Yandex Maps, 2GIS, websites and QR codes.
- Selection of a service, specialist and available time, with a waiting list for openings.
- Resource scheduling for rooms, chairs, equipment, vehicle bays or other industry needs.
- Customer self-service for confirming, moving or cancelling appointments under the business's rules.
- Booking-source tracking so owners can see where customers came from.

### Customer records and retention

- Customer cards with visit and purchase history, notes and consents.
- Telegram reminders, with SMS as a paid fallback when a customer has no Telegram.
- Bonuses, cashback, loyalty levels, birthday offers and referral rewards.
- Memberships, service courses and gift certificates.
- Targeted campaigns based on last visit, service intervals, booking source, spending or visit frequency.
- Review requests and feedback handling; the homepage also advertises a shared administrator view for Instagram Direct and reviews.

### Payments and point of sale

- Recording of cash, card-terminal and local digital payments, including payment options named on the homepage such as Alif QR, DC NEXT and Korti Milli.
- Split payments, prepayments, deposits, bonuses and customer debts.
- Opening and closing cash shifts, reconciliation and discrepancy reporting.
- Three branch-level fiscalization modes described in the FAQ: without fiscalization, recording a receipt number from an existing cash register, or using a connected online cash register.

The product terms describe bank acquiring agreements for accepting non-cash payments. Recording a payment method in the system and enabling payment processing are separate setup steps.

### Inventory and staff pay

- Stock balances, minimum-stock alerts and supplier orders.
- Automatic use of consumables according to a predefined service recipe, called a technological card on the website.
- Parts linked to repair or automotive work orders.
- Pay calculations using salary, service and product commissions, tips, KPIs and other configured adjustments.
- Staff access to their own earnings and payroll export to Excel.

### Reporting and multiple branches

- An owner dashboard with revenue, workload, customer activity, cash, debts and reviews.
- More than 20 advertised reports covering areas such as sales, customer retention, stock and payroll.
- Role-based access for owners, administrators and employees.
- A shared customer base and combined reporting across branches.

### Phones, browsers and offline work

The site describes Android and iOS apps for owners, staff and customers, plus browser access from phones, tablets and computers. A computer is not required for basic use.

The advertised offline scope includes schedules, customers, the till and stock, with automatic synchronization when the connection returns. The homepage states a target of a full shift, or eight hours. The FAQ says conflicting bookings made while disconnected are surfaced separately after synchronization, so offline conflict handling remains relevant even though other homepage copy promises no double bookings.

## Typical customer journey

1. A customer finds a booking link through social media, maps, a website or a QR code.
2. They choose a service, specialist and free time. The homepage describes phone-number-and-code access without a password.
3. They receive reminders, advertised for one day and two hours before the appointment, and can confirm or reschedule.
4. Staff record the arrival and complete the service. The receipt is built from the booking.
5. Payment, loyalty rewards, staff earnings and relevant inventory usage are recorded through the connected workflow.
6. Follow-up messages can request feedback or invite the customer to book again.

## Public customer catalog

NavCRM also has a [public catalog](https://navcrm.tj/catalog) where customers can search for businesses by city and category and enter a booking journey.

The catalog includes beauty, healthcare, fitness, education, automotive, repair, cleaning and wedding categories. Its city selector includes Dushanbe, Khujand, Bokhtar and other Tajik cities. During this review, the default Dushanbe view displayed **“No businesses in the catalog in this city yet.”** This observation does not establish the number of businesses using NavCRM privately or in other cities.

## Pricing and business model

NavCRM advertises a usage-based plan: **1.5 Tajik somoni (TJS) per completed visit**, with no subscription fee and no limits on employees, branches or features.

The product terms define a billable visit as an appointment or order marked attended or completed, or associated with a closed receipt. Cancelled appointments and no-shows are not charged.

| Item | Published terms |
| --- | --- |
| Completed visit | 1.5 TJS |
| Monthly subscription | None |
| Telegram notifications | Free |
| SMS | Charged separately from a prepaid balance at the operator's rate |
| Initial setup and customer migration | Free within the stated onboarding scope |
| Initial staff training | Up to two hours included |
| Travel outside Dushanbe, extra training and custom work | Separately agreed |
| Price protection | Rate fixed for 12 months from acceptance, according to the product terms |

Example monthly charges, excluding SMS and separately agreed services:

- 300 completed visits: **450 TJS**.
- 600 completed visits: **900 TJS**.
- 1,000 completed visits: **1,500 TJS**.

The terms describe the visit fee being deducted through a payment partner for non-cash payments and invoiced for other visits. They state that acquiring commission is included in the tariff. The “Start free” website button should not be read as a permanent free plan; the terms say promotional trial periods may be offered.

## Onboarding and support

The advertised onboarding process aims to get a business running in one day:

1. Select an industry profile and configure services and prices.
2. Import customers from Excel, a notebook or another system using NavTech's migration process.
3. Train the administrator and employees in approximately two hours.
4. Add booking entry points to social media, maps and a QR code.
5. Review the first month's bookings, no-shows, cash and payroll results.

Support is offered in Tajik and Russian through Telegram and telephone. The terms include training in Dushanbe or remotely; visits outside Dushanbe require a separate arrangement.

## Local focus and data

The product is tailored to Tajikistan through Tajik and Russian language support, local payment options, local support and offline workflows. The public website also offers an English interface.

NavTech states that customer and business data are stored in a data centre in Tajikistan. Customers can export their data to Excel. These are provider statements; hosting, security controls and data flows were not independently inspected for this overview.

## AI assistants and feature status

The homepage labels AI assistants **“Coming soon”**, while the product terms describe them as **beta**. Their general availability is therefore unclear from the public pages.

The described capabilities are:

- **Customer assistant:** understand Tajik, Russian or mixed-language messages in Telegram and Instagram Direct; answer routine questions and help book or move appointments.
- **Owner assistant:** answer questions about revenue, comparisons and staff workload using business data.
- **Staff assistant:** summarize a customer's history before a visit and add voice notes to customer records.

The website says health questions and disputed cases should be handed to an administrator. These AI capabilities should be treated as announced or beta functionality until their availability is confirmed.

## How to interpret the website's claims

The homepage uses sample businesses, booking screens and financial calculations to explain the product. It explicitly says that names and amounts in examples are illustrative.

Figures such as a 30–50% reduction in no-shows, payroll taking one hour instead of two days, or large annual savings are presented as targets or examples. They are not independently verified customer outcomes in this document.

This review covered public product information. It did not include a signed-in business account, a completed booking, installed mobile apps, payment processing or an offline test. No technology stack or internal architecture is inferred from the marketing pages.
